# TREATMENT — Pal-Pal (60s Spy Title Sequence Edition, 9:16)

## 1. Three Candidate Structures & Director's Choice

1. **Candidate A — Rooftop Pursuit (Linear Chase):** A trench-coated silhouette agent chases a courier across seven rooftop credit lines before catching a briefcase on the final word. *Rejected:* Feels generic to action spy films and ignores the romantic longing and intoxication of the *Pal Pal* lyrics.
2. **Candidate B — Countdown Dossier (Static Files):** Seven classified dossier folders stamp open one by one on a desk as each lyric line plays. *Rejected:* Too static and overlaps with Halftone Dossier rather than the kinetic cut-paper silhouette theatre of a 60s Spy Title Sequence.
3. **Candidate C — Nocturnal Spy-Romance Across Graphic Sets (CHOSEN):** A cut-paper 1960s neo-noir romance in four flat inks (`#1b1714` Ink Black, `#efe4c9` Paper Cream, `#d23a22` Vermilion Red, `#e2a52a` Mustard Gold). A trench-coated **Agent Silhouette** (with a streaming `#d23a22` red tie) wanders through seven graphic locations built out of the lyrics themselves, haunted by and pursuing the silhouette of a **Mystery Woman** (with a flowing `#e2a52a` mustard silk scarf) until she steps into a giant Mustard Gold circle on *"kar ke solah singhaar"* and the two silhouettes freeze together on the final stab.
   - *Why chosen:* Directly dramatizes every single Urdu/Hindi lyric line (*living each moment without you → pushing away useless drinks → transfixed by her eyes → refusing to go home, walking outside → waiting under the streetlamp → dreaming of her descent → her radiant arrival in full adornment*) while honoring every invariant of `styles/spy-titles/STYLE.md`.

## 2. Logline & Arc
- **Logline:** In a 1960s scissor-cut paper city of four flat inks, a restless secret agent is haunted by the silhouette of the woman he loves across clocks, cocktail lounges, keyholes, rain-slicked streets, and surreal dream stairs until she materializes inside a giant golden spotlight.
- **Arc:**
  - **Setup (`0.00s – 8.52s`, Intro + Lines 1–2):** Restless separation — the Agent paces across giant scissor-cut clock hands (*"Pal-pal jeena muhaal mera tere bina"*) and sweeps away cut-paper cocktail glasses at a tilted lounge bar (*"Yeh saaray nashay bekaar"*).
  - **Turn (`8.60s – 15.22s`, Lines 3–5):** Obsession & vigil — a graphic match cut into a giant scissor-cut eye reveals her silhouette (*"teri aankhon ke siwa"*); the Agent leaves his doorway for the streetlamp cone (*"Ghar nahi jaata, mein bahar"*) and keeps watch beneath her Venetian-blind window (*"Rehta tera intezaar"*).
  - **Ending (`15.32s – 20.00s`, Lines 6–7):** Dream & radiant union — she descends a floating surreal staircase into his dream (*"Mere khuwabon main aa naa"*), blossoms inside a giant Mustard Gold spotlight circle adorned in cut-paper jewels (*"kar ke solah singhaar"*), and the two silhouettes lock in a stop-time freeze frame.

## 3. Benchmark (Learn / Don't Learn)
- **References:** Saul Bass's title sequences for *Anatomy of a Murder* (1959) and *Vertigo* (1958); Kuntzel + Deygas's cut-paper sequence for *Catch Me If You Can* (2002).
- **What to learn:**
  - Flat, opaque four-ink paper cutouts with cached local-space scissor-cut edges (`9–10 px` segment resampling, normal displacement, occasional notches).
  - Merged character silhouettes with thin joint slits (shoulder, elbow, hip, knee) and a singleground-colour cut-gap outline so black figures read cleanly over black type.
  - Puppets animated on twos (`12 fps` stepped pose updates) against smooth `30 fps` camera/credit slides and diagonal strip shatters on musical cuts.
- **What not to take:** No gun barrels, no real film titles or logos, no gradients, no halftone dots.
